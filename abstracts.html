1. Tori and conjugacy in SL(2)

Speaker: Chen Cheng · November 3, 2026

The theory of endoscopy provides a framework for studying certain instances of Langlands functoriality. I will give a brief overview of the main themes of the seminar from the perspective of endoscopy. We will then turn to the case of SL(2), focusing on its tori and conjugacy classes.

2. Restriction from GL(2) to SL(2)

We examine the restriction of irreducible admissible representations of GL(2) to SL(2), focusing on the decomposition into constituents, multiplicity one, and self-twists. This provides a concrete route toward understanding packets of representations of SL(2).

3. L-packets and characters

We study L-packets and their characters for SL(2), with examples over real and nonarchimedean local fields. A central question is how characters of individual representations combine into stable distributions.

4. Orbital integrals and stability

We introduce ordinary, stable, and κ-orbital integrals through explicit conjugacy classes in SL(2). The example of two rational conjugacy classes within a single stable class motivates the weighted combinations that appear in endoscopic transfer.

5. Endoscopic tori for SL(2)

We describe endoscopic data for SL(2) using elliptic tori, including norm-one tori arising from quadratic extensions. The rank-one setting makes the general definitions concrete and provides the objects that will appear in transfer identities.

6. Transfer factors

We discuss the transfer factors associated with SL(2) and its endoscopic tori. Particular attention is given to the discriminant, sign, and normalization terms and to their role in comparing orbital integrals.

7. Endoscopic character identities

We consider the transfer of character distributions from an endoscopic torus to SL(2). In concrete examples, the resulting identities relate stable torus distributions to weighted combinations of characters within an L-packet.

8. Local transfer and the fundamental lemma

We explore a local transfer calculation for SL(2) and discuss the matching of orbital integrals. The unit fundamental lemma will be formulated in this setting, with emphasis on its meaning and its role in stabilizing the trace formula.

9. The trace formula for compact quotients

We introduce the trace formula in the compact-quotient case, beginning with the integral kernel of the regular representation. We then compare its geometric expansion over conjugacy classes with its spectral expansion in terms of representations.

10. Truncation and continuous spectrum

Passing to the noncompact case introduces divergent integrals and contributions from the continuous spectrum. We explain the purpose of truncation and outline the structure of the resulting geometric and spectral expansions.

11. Stabilization of the regular elliptic term

We examine how regular elliptic terms on the geometric side can be reorganized according to stable conjugacy. The discussion highlights the contributions of endoscopic tori and the role of local transfer in the passage to stable distributions.

12. The stable trace formula

We give an overview of the stabilization of the trace formula for SL(2), bringing together the local identities developed in earlier meetings. We conclude by discussing the spectral interpretation in terms of packets and multiplicities.
